# Case 2 - Phased Project Design - Looks eligible but isn’t

## Scenario

Raspberry Park is a fictional New Jersey shore town of about 6,000 year-round residents. The borough owns The Rabbits Center, an Art Deco style, 4-story brick-and-terra-cotta convention hall built in 1929 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The Center stands directly over the boardwalk, and is less than 50 feet from the ocean.

The Center is in a VE zone on the current Flood Insurance Rate Map, with a Base Flood Elevation of 13 feet (NAVD88). Its first floor is at 10 feet, and its basement holds the electrical switchgear, boilers, and the emergency generator. Seawater flooded the basement in a 2012 hurricane and again in a 2022 nor’easter. The 2022 event knocked out power to the building for nine days.

The borough has designated the Center as its post-storm supply distribution and recovery center. It’s in good standing with the NFIP and holds flood insurance on the Center. The site isn’t within a Coastal Barrier Resources System unit or an Otherwise Protected Area.

The borough administrator wants to “restore and harden The Rabbits Center for the next century” and “make the boardwalk worthy of it.” The borough plans to apply under the FY2024 & 2025 BRIC NOFO, with applications open March 25 – July 23, 2026. No construction has started on any project below.

### Facts

Raspberry Park Business Administrator thinks that this project will take up too much of the capacity of the engineering team and wants to implement a phased project. She would like to request for funding to complete design and permitting operations for this BRIC program cycle, and request funding for construction under a future cycle of the BRIC program.

Raspberry Park Business Adminstrator states: “We have previously applied for this grant, and were successfully awarded funding for a phased project. Surely FEMA does not exclusively award fully-designed, shovel-ready projects. Since they have accepted phased projects in the past, I don’t see why they wouldn’t accept them now. This project will be too much of a strain on our staff if we push to complete engineering and permitting design before the application deadline.”

## Governing text

Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO), for FY24 and FY25 “Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities”

Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)’s Hazard Mitigation Assistance (HMA) Program and Policy Guide, effective January 20, 2025

## Position

Phased projects are eligible because design and permitting are standard first phases of other construction projects that we have been approved for in the past.

Goal: Confirm in the guidelines if phased projects are allowable.

## Arguments

### Argument 1

The NOFO for this application cycle states phased projects are not eligible. (NOFO, pg 10)

### Argument 2

The program will fund “project scoping activities for specific infrastructure”, like the projects proposed specifically for The Rabbits Center. Design and permitting can be classified as project scoping work and should be eligible for funding. Otherwise, general project scoping activities, not tied to specific infrastructure, are not eligible for program funding.
