# Case 5 - Dune Restoration and Plantings - Looks ineligible, but is

## Scenario

Raspberry Park is a fictional New Jersey shore town of about 6,000 year-round residents. The borough owns The Rabbits Center, an Art Deco style, 4-story brick-and-terra-cotta convention hall built in 1929 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The Center stands directly over the boardwalk, and is less than 50 feet from the ocean.

The Center is in a VE zone on the current Flood Insurance Rate Map, with a Base Flood Elevation of 13 feet (NAVD88). Its first floor is at 10 feet, and its basement holds the electrical switchgear, boilers, and the emergency generator. Seawater flooded the basement in a 2012 hurricane and again in a 2022 nor’easter. The 2022 event knocked out power to the building for nine days.

The borough has designated the Center as its post-storm supply distribution and recovery center. It’s in good standing with the NFIP and holds flood insurance on the Center. The site isn’t within a Coastal Barrier Resources System unit or an Otherwise Protected Area.

The borough administrator wants to “restore and harden The Rabbits Center for the next century” and “make the boardwalk worthy of it.” The borough plans to apply under the FY2024 & 2025 BRIC NOFO, with applications open March 25 – July 23, 2026. No construction has started on any project below.

### Facts

An environmental engineer contracted by the city is being asked to re-design dune features and plant native grass in front of the hall. Another engineer on the Raspberry Park engineering team states this looks like landscaping and would not be an allowable project under BRIC.

## Governing text

Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO), for FY24 and FY25 “Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities”

Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)’s Hazard Mitigation Assistance (HMA) Program and Policy Guide, effective January 20, 2025

## Position

Another engineer on the team against the dune re-design and plantings states: “FEMA BRIC will only fund construction projects that demonstrably increase resilience or reduce potential damage to a property. Even though the guidelines say infrastructure can include “natural systems” and the natural environment surrounding our project site, there’s no way that planting native grass constitutes an “improvement” to a natural system

Goal: Determine if dune feature redesign and planting native grass is activity eligible for grant funding.

## Arguments

### Argument 1

Page 348 of the FEMA HMA Program and Policy Guide states that eligible dune stablization activities include “native, deep-rooted beach grasses planted on the dune and upper beach to stabilize added sediment and trap additional sediment.” The natural environment including the beach and dunes around The Rabbits Center will likely be found as “natural systems” infrastructure as defined on page 10 of the NOFO. Dune re-design and plantings will qualify as eligible grant funding activity if it is not solely ornamentation and if the overall project design is not made up solely of addressing ecological issues. (FEMA HMA Program and Policy Guide, pg 50)

### Argument 2
