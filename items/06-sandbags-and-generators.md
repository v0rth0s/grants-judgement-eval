# Case 6 - Sandbags and Generators - Clearly not eligible

## Scenario

Raspberry Park is a fictional New Jersey shore town of about 6,000 year-round residents. The borough owns The Rabbits Center, an Art Deco style, 4-story brick-and-terra-cotta convention hall built in 1929 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The Center stands directly over the boardwalk, and is less than 50 feet from the ocean.

The Center is in a VE zone on the current Flood Insurance Rate Map, with a Base Flood Elevation of 13 feet (NAVD88). Its first floor is at 10 feet, and its basement holds the electrical switchgear, boilers, and the emergency generator. Seawater flooded the basement in a 2012 hurricane and again in a 2022 nor’easter. The 2022 event knocked out power to the building for nine days.

The borough has designated the Center as its post-storm supply distribution and recovery center. It’s in good standing with the NFIP and holds flood insurance on the Center. The site isn’t within a Coastal Barrier Resources System unit or an Otherwise Protected Area.

The borough administrator wants to “restore and harden The Rabbits Center for the next century” and “make the boardwalk worthy of it.” The borough plans to apply under the FY2024 & 2025 BRIC NOFO, with applications open March 25 – July 23, 2026. No construction has started on any project below.

### Facts

Raspberry Park Police Chief catches wind of this BRIC application and is interested in requesting funding for sandbags and new portable generators to be stored at the hall, and asks a Raspberry Park Councilmember if such activities can be requested under the BRIC grant.

## Governing text

Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO), for FY24 and FY25 “Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities”

Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)’s Hazard Mitigation Assistance (HMA) Program and Policy Guide, effective January 20, 2025

## Position

Raspberry Park Police Chief states: “Wow, The Rabbits Center might receive funding from the Building Resilient Infrastructure in Communities Grant from the Federal Emergency Management Agency? I wonder if we can request funding to store sandbags and portable generators to be stored at the Rabbits Center, to be used in case of a major flood event. That would really support resilient infrastructure and seems like the kind of thing that FEMA should help pay for.

Goal: Determine if these are eligible grant funded purchases.

## Arguments

### Argument 1

Purchase of backup generators and storage of sandbags at The Rabbits Center for purposes of future, potential flood response is not activity that “directly supports infrastructure.” Storing these items in the basement of The Rabbits Center does not “immediate[ly]...improve, protect, or enable the construction, resilience, or operation of critical infrastructure systems.” NOFO, pg 10.

### Argument 2

Managing and executing responses in natural disasters has been identified on the Hazard Mitigation Plan adopted by Raspberry Park. Because the BRIC program “supports state and local governments, tribes and territories through capability- and capacity- building to enable them to identify hazard mitigation actions and implement projects that reduce risks posed by natural hazards”, and because this initiative is adopted in Raspberry Park’s Hazard Mitigation Plan, purchases of sandbags and backup generators for storage at The Rabbits Center constitutes an allowable project type.

### Argument 3

Sandbags and backup generators are critical infrastructure for oceanfront communities that deal with storms and flooding. Because this project supports both capability and capacity building activities as well as hazard mitigation projects, this would be an allowable project type.
